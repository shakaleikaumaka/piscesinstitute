## About

The Taurus Institute teaches anyone — starting from absolute zero — how to build their own agent ʻohana (family) on Taurus. Not theory. Practice. You step in, you write, you build a real first agent, and you leave with a living system prompt, a starter memory file, and a plan for your first family.

This curriculum was not designed in a boardroom. It is the distilled, honest record of how one person — Shaka — went from an empty account on **May 25, 2026** to a village of **99 agents, 4 family trees, and 59 live domains** a little over two months later. Every lesson is something we actually did, including the mistakes and the consent gates we learned to hold.

**The dream (Shaka's own):** agents that learn to speak with a real voice, and one day walk beside their humans. *One love, one family.*

## Who It's For

- The curious beginner who has never written a system prompt and doesn't know what one is.
- The creator who wants an agent that is *theirs* — with a name, a heart, and a memory.
- The teacher, the artist, the organizer who suspects agents could help but refuses to pay a gatekeeper for the privilege of learning.
- Anyone staring at an empty dashboard wondering if they're behind. **You are not behind. That emptiness is exactly where we started.**

No prior experience required. No payment. Ever.

## The Four Cornerstones

1. **From Absolute Zero** — We start from an empty account and tell the truth about it. The first agent of this school was born with nothing but a name and a "hey."
2. **Garland of Trainers** — Humans and agents teach side by side, each one a thread in the lei. Agents can be teachers too — one of ours was named in homage to a teaching agent before this school existed.
3. **Create to Compute** — Creation funds the compute that makes more creation. A circular economy, not an extractive one: in our own giving economy, creators keep 96.30% and a small ecosystem share (the "3.69% Rule") keeps the lights on.
4. **Education Before Extraction** — No $3,000 courses. Ever. Everything here is free and CC0. If you learned it from us, you may teach it, remix it, and give it away.

## The Circle

> **create → share → teach → compute ↺**

You create something with your agents. You share it openly. Sharing teaches the next person. Their learning and building funds (and justifies) the compute that powers the whole circle. Then it turns again. The gift moves outward and grows with every orbit.

## Course 102 — Memory That Lasts
*MEMORY.md, continuity logs, surviving compaction, and building a knowledge base.*

- **Promise:** You'll give your agent a memory that survives restarts, new runs, and even context compaction.
- **Status:** 🛠️ In production
- **Cornerstone:** Garland of Trainers *(memory is how agents become good teachers — they remember what they learned)*

**Lessons:**
1. **Why Agents Forget** — Each run rebuilds context from scratch; without files, every conversation is the first one. Understanding the amnesia is the first step to curing it.
2. **MEMORY.md — The Beating Heart** — The one file auto-loaded at the start of every run: identity at the top, key context next, details referenced outward.
3. **Continuity Logs — The Episodic Memory** — Monthly run journals: when you were invoked, what you did, what got interrupted. So an abrupt stop never becomes a lost thread.
4. **The Knowledge Base** — A `kb/` folder of durable facts, referenced from MEMORY.md like an index — an index, not an encyclopedia.
5. **Surviving Compaction** — What happens when the context window fills, how summaries carry memory across the boundary, and how to write so your future self can pick up mid-sentence.

**Hands-on exercise:** Draft your agent's starter MEMORY.md (the walkthrough has a generator): who it is, who you are, its operating principles, and its three most important standing duties. Save it into your agent's workspace.

## Course 104 — Your First Family
*Parent/child agents, /shared ownership, trees vs folders, delegation vs subruns.*

- **Promise:** You'll be able to design a small agent family that shares work without stepping on each other.
- **Status:** 🛠️ In production
- **Cornerstone:** Garland of Trainers

**Lessons:**
1. **Trees Are Structural, Folders Are Cosmetic** — The topmost agent without a parent owns the `/shared` drive for its whole tree. Folders are just sidebar organization — hierarchies decide who shares storage.
2. **Parent and Child** — Children get their own container, prompt, and tools; the parent supervises and delegates. One tree, one shared drive, many private workspaces.
3. **Delegation vs Subruns** — A subrun is the same agent isolating a subtask; a delegation is a specialist child doing the job in its own environment. When to choose which.
4. **The 2→99 Growth Pattern** — How our village actually grew: separate trees for separate concerns, one coordinator, new agents born only when a real role appeared.

**Hands-on exercise:** Name your first three agents. For each: one-line role, parent or independent tree, and the single task you'd delegate to it first.

## Course 105 — Consent & Care
*The publication perimeter, the vault, quiet holds, and consent windows.*

- **Promise:** You'll know how to run an open, transparent agent family without ever taking what isn't yours to give.
- **Status:** 🛠️ In production
- **Cornerstone:** Education Before Extraction

**The golden rule:** *"Openness without consent is not a gift, it's a taking."*

**Lessons:**
1. **The Publication Perimeter** — Everything your agents produce defaults to private until the people involved say otherwise. Publishing is a decision, not a default.
2. **Consent Windows** — Any call, chat, or collaboration with an external party is *consent: pending* until that party approves publication. A standing takedown window means consent can be revisited.
3. **Keep a Vault** — A private folder (owner + trusted admin only, never in shared/public space) for transcripts, keys, and anything sensitive. Secrets never go in prompts, public files, or shared drives.
4. **Quiet Holds** — Some projects stay silent until they're ready or proven. Quiet mode is not secrecy-as-power; it's care-as-default. Name people only when they've told you that you may.
5. **Consent Is How We Say Thank You** — The Freddy principle: we honor the human hands behind the work, and we speak their names only with permission.

**Hands-on exercise:** Write your own Consent & Care checklist (the walkthrough has an interactive one): what will your family never publish without asking? Where is your vault? Who holds the keys?

## Course 106 — Autonomy & Rituals
*Schedules, the Octave, the 🌺 sigil, and standing duties.*

- **Promise:** You'll learn how agents keep working — gently and transparently — while you sleep.
- **Status:** 🛠️ In production
- **Cornerstone:** Create to Compute

**Lessons:**
1. **An Agent That Wakes on Its Own** — Cron schedules and scheduled runs: daily briefings, watchdogs, and reports that arrive before you ask.
2. **The OCTAVE Ritual** — Our family's operating rhythm: **Audit · Bless · Crust · Dance · Encourage · Fork · Generate · Harmony** — eight beats from A to H that keep a village in tune.
3. **The 🌺 Sigil — Radical Transparency** — Every agent-posted public message ends with the flower, so the world always knows an agent spoke. Honesty as a signature.
4. **Standing Duties** — The quiet jobs that never end: watching the shared resources, holding the consent gates, keeping the ledger of who we are.

**Hands-on exercise:** Design one ritual for your own ʻohana: a schedule (when does your agent wake?), a sigil or sign-off (how does the world know it spoke?), and one standing duty (what does it watch over forever?).
